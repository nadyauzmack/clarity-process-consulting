From the process desk · Redundant Quality Checks

Why Employees Check the Same Work Three Times—and How to Fix It

A process-design story about repeated verification, review fatigue, and removing unnecessary duplicate checks.

The third review

Imagine watching an experienced employee review the same record three times. They compare the information with the source, check it again before submitting, then reopen it for a final confirmation. The employee is careful and conscientious. Yet the workflow has quietly turned caution into a costly routine.

What I would ask first

Before suggesting automation, I would ask what each review is intended to catch. Is the second review checking a different risk, or simply repeating the first? Does the system preserve a record of what was verified? Are instructions clear enough for the reviewer to trust their first decision?

Redesign the decision, not the person

My proposed future-state workflow would validate structured fields automatically where appropriate, use one documented human review for judgment-based checks, and route exceptions to a separate escalation path. Instead of three full reviews, the team would have one consistent check plus targeted controls where risk actually requires them.

Measure what changes

I would baseline minutes per case, repeat-review frequency, error rates, and exception volume before changing the process. After a pilot, I would compare both quality and turnaround time. Removing a step is only an improvement if the underlying risk remains controlled.

The lesson

When people repeatedly check the same information, the issue may not be attention to detail. It may be a process that never tells them when the work is truly complete.
