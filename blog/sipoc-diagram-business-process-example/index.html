SIPOC Diagram Example: How to Define a Business Process

Learn Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, and Customers with a realistic office process example and SIPOC mapping steps.

Educational guide: Illustrative scenarios are examples, not claims about specific client projects.

What SIPOC means

SIPOC stands for Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, and Customers. It provides a high-level picture of a process's purpose and boundaries before anyone draws a detailed workflow.

A SIPOC diagram is particularly useful when different departments describe the same process differently. It can reveal whether the confusion starts with missing inputs, unclear ownership, or an unrecognized customer.

Illustrative example: employee onboarding

Suppliers: recruiting, hiring managers, HR, and IT. Inputs: approved start date, role, equipment needs, manager, and location. Process: receive request, verify completeness, route tasks, set up access, and confirm readiness. Outputs: prepared access, equipment, and onboarding instructions. Customers: employees, managers, and supporting teams.

If new-hire location information is routinely missing, the solution may be an improved intake requirement—not another reminder to the person performing equipment setup.

How to run a SIPOC discussion

Invite people who provide information, perform the workflow, and use its outcome. Start by agreeing on the output and customer, then identify the key steps, inputs, and suppliers.

Stay at a high level. Five to seven broad process steps are often enough. Document disagreements so they can be resolved during detailed mapping.

Move from SIPOC to process design

Once boundaries are clear, map decisions, wait times, handoffs, and exceptions. Ask whether inputs can be validated earlier and whether every handoff has a named owner.
