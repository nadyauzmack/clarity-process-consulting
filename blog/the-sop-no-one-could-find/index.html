The SOP No One Could Find: A Knowledge Base Redesign Story

A narrative look at scattered SOPs, duplicate documentation, and designing a reliable source of truth.

Illustrative scenario: This first-person consulting perspective is a fictionalized example of how I would approach a common operational challenge. It is not a claim about a specific client or confidential workplace event.

The document exists—somewhere

Imagine an employee asking for a procedure and receiving three links: an old shared-drive file, a team chat attachment, and a newer document with no version date. The organization has plenty of documentation, but no reliable answer.

Find the actual friction

I would inventory the content people use most, identify duplicates, and observe how employees search for instructions. The problem may be naming, permissions, ownership, outdated material, or all four.

Design around tasks

Instead of organizing the knowledge base around the company org chart, I would organize it around what employees need to do. Each procedure would have a clear title, owner, review date, and related workflow. Archived versions would remain traceable without competing with current guidance.

Keep it alive

The new system needs a publishing process: who can propose a change, who approves it, and when it is reviewed. Otherwise a clean knowledge base becomes cluttered again.

The lesson

Documentation is only useful when people can find the right answer and trust that it is current.
