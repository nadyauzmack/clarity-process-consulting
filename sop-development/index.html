SOP development · Knowledge management

Procedures people can find, trust and actually use.

Standard operating procedures should explain the work—not become another folder employees avoid. Build current, usable instructions with clear owners and exception handling.

The difference between having SOPs and having a knowledge system

Documentation fails when it is inconsistent, impossible to search, or disconnected from real work. A great SOP tells a person where to start, which inputs to check, which decisions they own, where exceptions go and when the process is complete.

Knowledge management makes those procedures accessible, organized and maintainable. For a remote team, that might mean improving a SharePoint library, reorganizing shared documentation, creating role-based guidance or establishing a clearer approval and revision process.

Experience building documentation at enterprise scale

In corporate operations I created more than 50 training and process resources across improvement work and helped build and maintain a SharePoint knowledge system. I've delivered knowledge transfer across six teams, learning what different roles need from the same process.

That experience informs how I organize job aids, role guides, step-by-step instructions, cross-team handoffs, checklists and knowledge-base structures. I don't assume one giant document will solve a communication problem.
